# Group scheduler, a better WhenIsGood

The scheduler is a way for friends to pick a time for a social event: an organizer posts a
few candidate times, each friend confirms theirs from a phone, and the tool does the chasing
of whoever has not answered. It is built in this repo beside the game and reuses the
server's accounts, deadline sweeper and notification door. Two problems, and they are
different ones: sharing availability is too much work (the grid is the friction), and the
organizer ends up chasing the last one or two people (the poke is the friction).

What would make it real: five friends get one link in the group chat, each marks their
availability on a phone in under thirty seconds, and the last holdout answers after the tool
pokes them, without the organizer sending a single follow-up text. That is the same shape as
the repo's own spec sentence, which is part of why it fits here. One part of that shape did
not survive: Q4 and Q5 put an account and a sign-in in front of the first answer, so the
"without an account" half is given up on purpose.

## What WhenIsGood does, and does not

The reference product is WhenIsGood (`whenisgood.net`). Direct fetch of the site was blocked
by the sandbox egress proxy, so everything here comes from search-result summaries, not the
site itself. The organizer creates a date and time grid and shares a link, and respondents
paint the slots they are free. It is free and has no accounts; a $20 a year tier removes ads
and adds Excel export. It stops at collecting availability: no calendar invite, no payment,
no reminders at all, and thin timezone handling. It has run the same way for well over a
decade. So the gap is real and specific: WhenIsGood has no poke.

## Plans that change with headcount

A poll's outcome can be conditional on how many commit: each candidate time shows its
confirmed count and what that count unlocks ("3 -> Catan, 5+ -> Werewolf"). Decided for v1
as organizer-typed tiers (Q10). It matters beyond convenience for two reasons:

- It is a poke. "One more and Saturday becomes Werewolf night" makes the holdout pivotal,
  which is a sharper lever than "4 of 5 have answered." It names what the group gains from
  them rather than what they are failing to do.
- It ties back to this repo. The repo's own games run at different headcounts (the balance
  gate sweeps 2/4/6/8/12 players), so a scheduler that knows the confirmed count could
  suggest one of them. That is a link, not a dependency.

## Poke levers

Five levers survived the decisions: progress or social proof ("4 of 5 are in"), naming the
holdouts to the whole group (Q9), peer nudges that arrive from a named friend (Q9), the
pivotal-person line when a headcount tier is one person away (Q10), and deadline escalation
as an optional deadline nears. Default-with-an-out, where silence is read as a yes, was
dropped by Q8: only an explicit answer counts.

## Assumptions still standing

- The group is small, 2 to 12, and everyone is reachable through the organizer's group chat.
- These are social events among friends, not work meetings, so no calendar-sync integration
  is needed for v1. The standing availability profile from Q6 stands in for it until the
  phone app.
- "Effective psychology" means the poke should get a reply, not maximize engagement. It must
  not feel like a dark pattern to a friend.

## Route

The order is set by risk, not by the order a user meets the features. Everything after the
Decisions table hangs on one assumption from Q2: that a push notification reliably reaches a
friend's phone from a web app, including an iPhone that has added the site to its home screen.
Nothing in this repo has ever sent a push. So the first piece of work is the push channel
itself, and nothing about polls is built until a nudge has visibly buzzed a real iPhone and a
real Android phone.

That first piece is worth having even if the scheduler stopped there, which is why it is safe to
start with: the game's own turn reminders and "turn resolved" mail (`tick.ts:151`,
`resolve.ts:140`) would arrive as pushes too. Concretely it is a web app manifest and a service
worker in `packages/web`, Firebase Cloud Messaging on the same Firebase project, device tokens
stored on the user doc as an optional field (old user docs have none, and every reader treats
absence as "no devices"), and a push transport inside `notify()` beside the email one, so the
per-kind preferences and the one-door rule in `notify.ts:64-69` cover pushes without a second
gate. The iPhone needs an Add to Home Screen walkthrough, because without it iOS never offers
the permission prompt at all. Testing needs HTTPS on a real phone, so it rides a Firebase
Hosting preview channel against the existing Cloud Run service rather than relighting the
mothballed site.

If the push does not arrive reliably on an iPhone, stop and reopen Q2 before building anything
else: the organizer-sent prefilled `sms:` links are the rejected option most likely to come back.

With pushes proven, the poll comes next, as its own `polls` collection rather than a fourth
member of the `GameDoc` union in `store.ts:90`. A poll has no seats, turns, seed or redaction,
and forcing it into the game base type would make every game reader learn to skip it. It shares
users, auth and `notify()`, and nothing else. Its code lives in `packages/server` and
`packages/web` rather than a new package, which keeps CI as it is: the changed-areas table
already runs server tests for server changes and skips the mapgen and balance sweeps unless the
engine or tools change. The poll needs one route outside the `RequireAuth` wall in
`packages/web/src/main.tsx:29`, so that a friend opening the link sees the poll's title and who
asked before the sign-in button (Q5). Answering is Yes/No per candidate time. The organizer locks
the final time by hand.

The availability profile comes third, as another optional field on the user doc, a weekly
pattern of free and busy blocks. It is filled from a person's first answers rather than by a
setup screen, so the bigger first-time ask from Q6 never appears as a form: answer one poll and
the next one arrives pre-ticked. Pre-ticks are only ever suggestions (Q8), and a poll records
nothing until the person confirms.

Headcount tiers follow, because they are only text and a threshold on the poll, and their
payoff is in the pokes, which come last. A poke is composed by one pure function of the poll and
the recipient, with no clock or I/O in it, so the whole psychology layer is unit-testable the way
the engine is: progress ("4 of 5 are in"), naming who is missing, the pivotal-person line when a
tier is one person away, and the deadline. The app's own pokes are driven by the existing
minute tick in `tick.ts:39`, escalating as an optional deadline nears. Peer nudges are a POST
from a friend, rate-limited on the poll document at one per friend per holdout per day, and
delivered as from that friend.

Two things happen at the end of the web phase rather than along the way. `CLAUDE.md` gets the
scheduler's own spec sentence beside the game's, and the site is relit by following the
mothball runbook in `docs/deployment.md` in reverse. The phone app is a separate project after
that, on the same accounts: Expo, Sign in with Apple alongside Google (App Store review requires
both if Google is offered), and the two features held back for native, reading the phone's
calendar to pre-fill the profile and Yes/No buttons on the iPhone notification itself.

## Superseded in part (2026-10-02)

Availability now comes from each member's Google Calendar or a painted grid, a poll is a date
range with a heatmap rather than organizer-typed candidate times, and the profile learned from
Yes/No answers is replaced by a remembered weekly pattern. Q4's deferral of calendar reading,
Q7, Q16 and the "no calendar-sync" assumption above are overturned there, with the reasons:
[calendar-availability.md](calendar-availability.md).
