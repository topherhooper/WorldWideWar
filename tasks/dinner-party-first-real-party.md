# Play one real dinner party, with real children

## Next step

Open the site, pick **Bedtime Party**, put the link in the family chat, and play one
Sleeping Beauty with actual guests. The smallest real run is two grown-ups and a child under
seven, which is now a supported size; the full hunt needs at least six adults to be worth it.
Do not tune any number first.

Watch for six things, in this order of interest:

1. **Does a child do the pretending unprompted**, or does it only happen because a grown-up
   asks for the clue? The whole design assumes the favour is the fun part rather than a toll.
2. **Does anybody read the encounter lists out loud?** That is the only route to catching the
   curser, and nothing in the UI teaches it.
3. **Do the candles and the floor timer feel right?** Every number in this is a guess.
4. **Is two voices for a duo too many, or not enough to notice?**
5. **Does a guest actually complete a Google sign-in standing in a hallway?** This is the one
   thing the four-letter code did better, and the risk the whole port took on. `dependents`
   is the escape hatch — anyone whose phone will not cooperate gets seated on somebody
   else's — but it has never been used by a person who did not write it.
6. **Does an invitation dealt on Tuesday still make sense on Saturday?** Nobody has held a
   role for four days before playing it.

## What we know

The machine works and the puzzle is fair — 3600 parties solvable, falsehoods that cannot
silently convict an innocent, and now 75 engine tests and a whole party end to end against
the emulator. None of that says whether an evening of it is fun, and no further simulation
will.
